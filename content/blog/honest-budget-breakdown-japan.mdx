Japan has a reputation for being expensive. Like most travel myths, it's half true. Yes, a high-end ryokan with kaiseki dinner can cost £400 per night. But a bowl of ramen costs £6, a night in a clean capsule hotel is £25, and the Shinkansen — the famous bullet train — is genuinely good value if you buy a JR Pass in advance.

Here's what 2 weeks in Japan actually cost, based on the most common route: **Tokyo → Hakone → Kyoto → Osaka**, with a day trip to Nara.

---

## The Big Ticket Item: The JR Pass

Buy it before you leave home. A **14-day nationwide JR Pass costs approximately £410** (prices change with the yen). It covers:
- All Shinkansen (except the Nozomi and Mizuho — Hikari is nearly as fast)
- Most local JR lines within cities
- The airport express to/from Tokyo (Narita) and Osaka (Kansai)

If you're doing Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka–back to Tokyo, a quick calculation shows the pass pays for itself on those journeys alone.

> Skip the pass if you're only visiting one region. For the classic multi-city route, it's a no-brainer.

## Accommodation Costs

Japan has a wider range of accommodation than almost anywhere else on earth.

| Type | Cost per night | Best for |
|------|----------------|---------|
| Capsule hotel | £20–35 | Solo travellers, budget-first |
| Business hotel (Toyoko Inn, Dormy) | £50–80 | Couples, comfort on a budget |
| Airbnb / apartment | £60–120 | Groups, longer stays |
| Traditional ryokan (budget) | £70–120 pp | Cultural experience |
| Ryokan with meals (luxury) | £200–400 pp | Splurge night |

**For 2 weeks, expect to spend £350–700 on accommodation** (solo, mix of capsule and business hotels). Couples splitting a room can bring this down to £250–400 each.

## Food: Where the Savings Hide

This is where Japan surprises people. If you eat like a local, Japan is remarkably affordable.

### Cheap eats (under £8 per meal)

- **Ramen**: £6–9 at dedicated ramen shops. Regional variations are wildly different — try tonkotsu in Osaka, shoyu in Tokyo.
- **Gyudon (beef bowls)**: Sukiya and Yoshinoya serve huge bowls from £3.50
- **Convenience stores**: Lawson and 7-Eleven are not a compromise — they're an institution. Onigiri, sandwiches, hot snacks: £2–4 per item, all fresh.
- **Soba and udon**: standing noodle bars near train stations serve a bowl for under £5
- **Izakaya happy hours**: 17:00–19:00 sees beer for £2 and yakitori skewers from £1.50

### Pricier but worth it

- Omakase sushi: £60–120 for an unforgettable lunch. Book via TableCheck or Tableall.
- Kobe beef: one proper meal (150g sirloin, set menu) costs £60–100. Worth saving for.

**Realistic food budget**: £30–45/day for someone eating well and splurging occasionally.

## The Things Worth Splashing On

Budget travel in Japan doesn't mean missing out on the best things. These are the splurges I'd always recommend:

1. **One night in a ryokan with breakfast and dinner included** — the full experience, tatami rooms, yukata robes, futon on the floor. Budget £120–180 per person for a decent one in Hakone or Kyoto.

2. **Hiroshima + Miyajima day trip** — the Peace Memorial Park and Museum is one of the most affecting places in the world. The ferry to Miyajima's floating torii gate takes 10 minutes.

3. **One proper sushi counter** — not a conveyor belt, a proper counter where the chef places each piece in front of you. Lunch sets start at £40 and are very much worth it.

## What to Skip

- **Taxis**: almost never necessary with Japan's exceptional public transit
- **Convenience store alcohol mark-ups**: buy from vending machines or drug stores (kombini alcohol is fine, just shop around)
- **Rush-hour reserved Shinkansen seats**: the unreserved carriages (自由席) on a JR Pass are fine outside peak times

## Saving Money Tips That Actually Work

**Eat breakfast at your hotel or convenience store** — hotel breakfasts in Japan are often enormous and included in the rate. A 7-Eleven coffee and onigiri costs under £3.

**Walk between tourist sites** — Kyoto's main temples are walkable from each other. Renting a bike is £5–8/day.

**Buy a Suica or Pasmo IC card** — pre-loaded transit card that works on almost every bus and subway in Japan. Faster and cheaper than buying individual tickets.

**Avoid weekends at famous spots** — weekday mornings at Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama bamboo, and Senso-ji are completely different experiences to Saturday afternoon chaos.

---

## Final Verdict

Two weeks in Japan for under £2,500 is very doable if you're comfortable with capsule hotels and eating at counters. For a more comfortable trip, budget £3,000–3,500. Japan rewards research — the more you know before you go, the more you'll get out of it.
