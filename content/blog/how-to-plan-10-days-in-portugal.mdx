Portugal punches well above its weight for first-time visitors and seasoned travellers alike. In 10 days you can hit the trifecta: Lisbon's hills and tiles, Porto's riverside wine cellars, and the Algarve's sea-stack beaches — without ever feeling rushed.

## Why 10 Days Works So Well

Shorter trips force you to pick one city. Longer trips invite over-planning. Ten days is the sweet spot: you can follow a **Lisbon → Porto → Algarve** route, travel slowly enough to feel the rhythm of each place, and still have a night train or budget flight connecting it all.

## The Route

### Days 1–3: Lisbon

Fly into Lisbon (LIS). The airport is 20 minutes from the centre by Metro.

**Day 1** — Recover from travel, walk Alfama. Catch Tram 28 for the views, eat a pastel de nata at Pastéis de Belém, and find a fado bar in Mouraria for the evening.

**Day 2** — Take the train to Sintra (40 minutes, €5 return). Pena Palace and the Moorish castle are both unmissable. Back in Lisbon, watch the sun set from the Miradouro da Graça.

**Day 3** — LX Factory market if it's a Sunday. Otherwise, the Museu do Azulejo is worth 2 hours. Afternoon: the stunning Jerónimos Monastery in Belém.

> **Budget tip**: A 24-hour Lisbon Metro pass is £5.50. Buy a Viva Viagem card at any Metro station.

### Days 4–5: Porto

Take the Alfa Pendular train from Lisbon Santa Apolónia (3 hours, from £18 booked ahead). Porto's old town, Ribeira, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for good reason.

**Day 4** — Walk the Ribeira waterfront. Cross the Dom Luís I Bridge on foot. Climb the Clérigos Tower for panoramic views. Wine tasting at one of the lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia — Graham's and Taylor's both offer excellent tours.

**Day 5** — Day trip to the Douro Valley. Either rent a car or join a tour. You'll pass terraced vineyards, ancient quintas, and tiny villages. An afternoon boat trip on the river is optional but unforgettable.

> **Money note**: Porto is noticeably cheaper than Lisbon. Expect to spend £50–70/day including accommodation.

### Days 6–7: Travel South + Algarve Base

Fly Porto → Faro (Ryanair, from £20 booked early). Alternatively, take the train south — it's longer but scenic.

Base yourself in **Lagos** (backpacker-friendly, stunning beaches) or **Tavira** (quieter, more local feel).

### Days 8–10: Algarve Coast

**Day 8** — Praia de Dona Ana and the sea caves at Ponta da Piedade. Kayak tours leave from Lagos beach and take about 2 hours. Worth every penny.

**Day 9** — Drive or bus to **Sagres**, the southwestern tip of Europe. The fortress here is staggering. Visit Praia do Beliche, one of the last uncrowded beaches on this coast.

**Day 10** — Slow morning, local market, flight home from Faro (FAO).

## Getting Around

- **City to city**: Alfa Pendular trains (Lisbon–Porto) and budget flights (Porto–Faro) are both excellent
- **Within Lisbon and Porto**: Metro + trams
- **Algarve**: Rent a car for maximum flexibility — prices drop to £18–25/day outside peak season

## Packing Notes

Portugal in spring and autumn (best times to visit) means 18–24°C days and cooler evenings. Bring layers. Comfortable walking shoes are non-negotiable — Lisbon and Porto are notoriously hilly.

## Final Thoughts

Portugal is rarely disappointing. The food is excellent and affordable, the people are warm, and the country has invested heavily in tourism infrastructure without losing its soul. This 10-day route works for first-timers and repeat visitors alike — and if you fall in love with it (you will), the Alentejo wine region and the Azores islands are waiting for trip two.
