Southeast Asia is the region that turns people into travellers. The food is extraordinary, the landscapes are absurd, and the culture is unlike anywhere in Europe. And yes — £50/day is more than enough to do it well.

This isn't about sacrifice. £50/day in SEA means street food that blows your mind, guesthouses with pools, and long-tail boat trips to beaches you'll see in your dreams. It's not about cutting corners; it's about knowing where the money goes furthest.

## The £50 Daily Budget Breakdown

| Category | Daily spend | Notes |
|----------|------------|-------|
| Accommodation | £10–16 | Fan room in guesthouse or dorm in a quality hostel |
| Food & drink | £12–18 | 3 meals street food, coffee, 2–3 beers |
| Local transport | £3–6 | Tuk-tuks, local buses, scooter rental |
| Activities | £5–12 | Temple entry, boat trips, cooking class spread over days |
| Buffer / misc | £3–5 | SIM data, water, snacks, tips |

On this budget you're eating better than you would at home, sleeping somewhere clean and often charming, and doing at least one activity per day.

## Where to Focus Your Time

### Thailand

Thailand is the gateway for most first-timers and remains the best introduction to the region. It's well-set-up for travellers without feeling corporate about it.

**Bangkok** — 3–4 days minimum. The temples (Wat Pho, Wat Arun, the Grand Palace) are unmissable. Eat on Khao San Road once for the atmosphere, then eat everywhere else for the food. A plate of pad see ew from a street cart costs £1.50.

**Chiang Mai** — 4–5 days. Slower, cooler, surrounded by mountains. Visit on a Sunday for the Night Bazaar. Take a half-day Thai cooking class (£15–20, includes a market trip and lunch). Day trips to Doi Inthanon National Park or the Long Neck Karen villages.

**Islands** — Pick one cluster. Koh Samui is the most developed; Koh Phangan and Koh Tao (world-class diving) are better value and less commercialised. Koh Lanta in the south is quieter still.

> **Budget reality check**: Thailand is slightly pricier than Vietnam and Cambodia. You'll need to be more intentional about avoiding tourist-trap restaurants near major sights.

### Vietnam

Vietnam is the most rewarding country in SEA for slow travel. It's long, it's varied, and the food is exceptional.

**Hanoi** — 3 days. The Old Quarter is chaotic and brilliant. Eat bun cha where Obama ate bun cha (Bun Cha Huong Lien, Hang Dieu). A motorbike tour of the city costs £12.

**Ha Long Bay** — Do the 2-day/1-night cruise. Budget tours start at £50pp and have improved significantly in quality. The landscape is one of the best things you'll ever see.

**Hoi An** — Everyone's favourite. Tailor-made clothes, white-walled lanes, the beach 5km away. Give it 3–4 days. A set of well-made linen trousers costs £18.

**Ho Chi Minh City** — 2–3 days. The War Remnants Museum is sober and essential. The street food scene in District 1 and District 3 is exceptional.

### Cambodia

Cambodians are warm, the food is underrated (lok lak, fish amok, nom banh chok), and Angkor Wat alone justifies the country.

**Siem Reap** — 3–4 days just for the temples. A 3-day Angkor pass costs £50 and includes access to all temple complexes. Hire a tuk-tuk driver for £12–15/day — a good one will take you to the temples that avoid the crowds.

**Phnom Penh** — 2 days. The Killing Fields and Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum are difficult but important. The riverside is livelier in the evenings.

## Getting Between Countries

| Route | Best option | Cost | Time |
|-------|------------|------|------|
| Bangkok → Chiang Mai | Overnight sleeper train | £12–20 | 13 hrs |
| Chiang Mai → Bangkok → Siem Reap | Budget flight | £40–80 | 2 hrs flying |
| Siem Reap → Ho Chi Minh City | Bus (border crossing) | £12 | 8–10 hrs |
| Ho Chi Minh City → Hanoi | Open-bus or flights | £15–50 | 2 hrs flying |

**Overnight trains and buses are your friends** — they save you a night's accommodation cost and you arrive fresh.

## The Things Worth Spending More On

Sticking to £50/day doesn't mean being rigid every day. These are the moments to loosen the budget:

- **Ha Long Bay cruise** (£50–80 for 2 days/1 night) — the standard tours have improved. Don't skip this to save £30.
- **Scuba diving in Koh Tao** — £250–300 for an Open Water PADI certification, or £30–40 for a fun dive. One of the cheapest places in the world to learn.
- **Cooking class in Hoi An** — £25–35, usually includes a market tour and 5+ dishes. The skills come home with you.
- **Angkor Wat sunrise** — free with your temple pass, wake up at 4:30am. Non-negotiable.

## Practical Budget Tips

**Eat where the locals eat** — If the menu has photographs and English translations prominently displayed, add 40% to your bill. The real food is at the places without menus — point at what others are having.

**Negotiate tuk-tuks but fairly** — Aim for a fair price, not the cheapest possible. A tuk-tuk driver working a full day earns £8–12. Negotiate down from opening price by 30%, then accept it gracefully.

**Get a local SIM card at the airport** — In Thailand (True Move, AIS), Vietnam (Viettel, Vietnamobile), and Cambodia (Smart, Metfone), you'll get 15–30GB of data for under £5. This alone saves significant money on roaming.

**Use ATMs attached to major banks** — Bangkok Bank, Vietcombank, Acleda Bank. Avoid standalone ATMs in tourist areas — higher fees and occasional skimming.

**Book accommodation the night before, not weeks ahead** — Except over major holidays (Songkran, Chinese New Year), turning up gives you leverage to inspect the room and negotiate.

## What a £50/Day SEA Trip Actually Feels Like

You're eating bánh mì for breakfast (£1), hiring a scooter for the day (£5), swimming at a beach that doesn't have a Starbucks (free), eating fresh seafood grilled on the beach (£8), and drinking a beer watching the sun set over the Mekong (£1.50). 

You are not roughing it. You are living better than you do at home.

Southeast Asia on a budget is not a compromise — it's a different way of travelling. Slower, more local, more memorable.
